ReTerra — Minecraft Datapack Concept

Project: ReTerra
Type: Survival / World Restoration / Progression
Platform: Minecraft Java Edition
Format: Datapack (optional resource pack)
Status: Planning

1. Main Concept

The player starts in a completely dead Minecraft world.

There are no living forests, grass, flowers, crops, or animals. Rivers have mostly dried up, villages are abandoned, and the land consists of dirt, coarse dirt, gravel, and exposed stone.

However, normal Minecraft resources such as wood, stone, ores, and minerals still exist.

Main objective: Restore the world, one biome at a time.

The player must collect resources, create fertile soil, establish farms, rediscover plants, restore ecosystems, and eventually bring animals back.

Restored biomes should never look exactly like vanilla Minecraft. Permanent signs of the catastrophe remain.

2. World Generation

Terrain

Normal Minecraft terrain shapes.

Ground consists mainly of dirt, coarse dirt, gravel, and exposed stone.

No naturally generated grass blocks.

No flowers, bushes, or living vegetation.

Some exposed rock and damaged terrain.

Underground generation remains mostly normal.

Caves, ores, and minerals are still obtainable.

Trees

Trees generate without leaves.

Some trees are broken or fallen.

Dead trees still provide normal wood.

Different dead tree types appear in their corresponding former biomes.

Water

Rivers are mostly dried up.

Riverbeds remain recognizable.

Small residual pools of water remain.

Oceans and large lakes could be partially depleted.

Water can eventually be restored through progression.

Structures

Villages are abandoned and partly ruined.

Farms contain no growing crops.

Structures that normally contain life are empty or destroyed.

Some structures contain surviving seeds or biological resources.

New abandoned laboratories, greenhouses, and seed vaults can generate.

Mobs

No naturally spawning passive animals.

No fish, bees, or other wildlife.

No villagers in abandoned settlements.

Hostile mobs still spawn.

Animals are reintroduced later through restoration.

Biomes

Original biome identities are preserved.

Every original biome has a corresponding dead version.

Biomes can be restored individually.

Reclaimed versions have their own appearance and environmental properties.

3. Main Progression

Stage 1 — Survival

Goal: Survive in the wasteland and discover the first signs of life.

Player activities:

Collect dead wood.

Craft normal tools.

Mine stone, coal, iron, and other resources.

Find remaining water.

Explore caves and abandoned buildings.

Search for mushrooms, seeds, and preserved biological materials.

Unlocks:

Basic compost.

Basic soil processing.

First crop seeds.

First grass culture.

Stage 2 — Soil Restoration

Goal: Create enough fertile ground to establish a small farm.

Player activities:

Collect coarse dirt and gravel.

Produce organic compost.

Convert coarse dirt into regular dirt.

Process gravel into new soil.

Build the first Soil Reclaimer.

Restore small patches of ground.

Unlocks:

Renewable soil production.

Small-scale terrain restoration.

Farmland preparation.

Stage 3 — First Growth

Goal: Grow the first crops and bring grass back.

Player activities:

Plant preserved seeds.

Establish irrigation.

Harvest wheat, potatoes, carrots, and beetroot.

Use crop leftovers for compost.

Create the first grass block using preserved grass culture.

Allow grass to spread across restored soil.

Unlocks:

Renewable food production.

Advanced compost.

Larger restoration areas.

New organic materials.

Stage 4 — Rewilding

Goal: Restore natural vegetation across the biome.

Player activities:

Find preserved saplings.

Grow the first living trees.

Establish multiple plant species.

Restore dried riverbeds.

Expand grasslands and forests.

Improve biodiversity.

Unlocks:

Larger Restoration Cores.

New vegetation.

Advanced soil processing.

Animal restoration technology.

Stage 5 — Ecosystem Recovery

Goal: Restore a stable, self-sustaining biome.

Player activities:

Discover preserved animal samples.

Restore suitable habitats.

Reintroduce animals.

Establish breeding populations.

Complete the biome's restoration requirements.

Result:

Biome is marked as Reclaimed.

Wildlife can reproduce and return naturally.

Vegetation becomes self-sustaining.

Restoration machines are no longer required.

Permanent environmental damage remains visible.

4. Soil System

Soil is one of the most important resources.

Ground Types

Gravel

Common in dead biomes.

Cannot grow crops.

Can be processed into soil using organic material.

Coarse Dirt

Very common in dead biomes.

Can be converted to normal dirt using a hoe.

Can also be processed by restoration machines.

Dirt

Exists naturally, but is limited in some regions.

Can support farmland and grass.

Can be produced using restoration systems.

Grass Block

Does not naturally generate in the starting world.

Must initially be created using preserved grass culture.

Can spread onto suitable nearby dirt.

Important for restoring vegetation and habitats.

Compost Production

Initial materials:

Dead wood / wood chips.

Mushrooms.

Bones / bone meal.

Other organic resources.

Later materials:

Wheat.

Crop leftovers.

Leaves.

Seeds.

Other plant products.

Renewable Soil Production

Possible custom recipe chain:

Gather dead wood and mushrooms.

Produce basic compost.

Combine gravel with organic compost to create coarse soil.

Refine coarse soil into usable dirt.

Use dirt for farmland or ecosystem restoration.

As crop production increases, compost and soil become easier to produce.

5. Farming System

Farming becomes the foundation of restoration progression.

Early Game

Seeds are rare.

Seeds must be found in abandoned structures.

Water is limited.

Only a small farm is possible.

Mid Game

Crops become renewable.

More farmland becomes available.

Compost production increases.

Different crops unlock new restoration materials.

Late Game

Large-scale farming becomes possible.

Crop products power advanced restoration processes.

Organic materials can be produced automatically.

Crop Uses

Wheat

Food: Bread.

Restoration: Straw mulch and compost.

Beetroot

Food: Beetroot soup.

Restoration: Nutrient-rich cultures.

Potatoes

Food: Baked potatoes.

Restoration: Fermented biological fuel.

Carrots

Food: Carrots.

Restoration: Biological processing and animal recovery.

Pumpkins / Melons

Food and crafting.

Restoration: Large amounts of compost material.

Main Resource Loop

Crops → Compost → Fertile Soil → More Crops → Larger Restoration Areas

Different ecosystems also require exploration resources, so farming alone cannot complete the entire game.

Purify small sections of terrain.

Prepare ground for grass and crops.

Possible construction:

Composter.

Furnace.

Stone or cobblestone.

Water.

Compost.

Fuel.

Suitable ground.

Correct multiblock arrangement.

Operating modes:

Production Mode:

Processes resources into dirt.

Outputs usable soil items.

Restoration Mode:

Slowly converts suitable surface blocks into dirt.

Consumes water and compost.

Only restores a limited radius.

Environmental values:

Soil fertility.

Water availability.

Biodiversity.

Environmental purity.

Better conditions increase restoration progress.

Machine 3 — Water Restoration System

Purpose:

Machine 4 — Biological Archive / Revival Chamber

7. Biome Restoration System

Biomes are restored individually, not globally.

How It Works

Player enters a dead biome.

Player constructs a Restoration Core.

Core registers a restoration region.

Player improves environmental conditions.

Core gradually restores nearby terrain sectors.

Restoration expands within the registered biome.

Biome reaches its final Reclaimed stage.

Player moves to another biome.

Sector System

Divide regions into approximately 16×16-block sectors.

Each sector stores restoration progress.

Terrain is changed gradually.

Only loaded sectors are processed.

Player builds and important structures should be protected.

Sector progress persists when the game is saved.

Biome Completion Requirements

Possible conditions:

A sufficient amount of terrain restored.

Healthy soil established.

Grass coverage established.

Multiple plant species growing.

Water availability restored.

Stable food production.

Wildlife reintroduced where appropriate.

Completion should depend on actual environmental improvements rather than simply donating enormous quantities of items.

8. Reclaimed Biomes

Fully restored biomes should NOT become normal vanilla biomes.

They should look alive, but permanently affected by the catastrophe.

Reclaimed Plains

Green meadows.

Flowers and scattered bushes.

Small tree groves.

Permanent gravel patches.

Occasional dead trees.

Partially restored riverbeds.

Slightly muted grass colors.

Reclaimed Forest

Living trees growing among dead trees.

Fallen logs.

Moss and mushrooms.

Uneven vegetation density.

Damaged ground patches.

Wildlife returning.

Reclaimed Swamp

Restored wetlands.

Muddy ground.

Shallow water.

Dead tree remains.

New swamp vegetation.

Some permanently dried areas.

Reclaimed Highlands

Patches of alpine vegetation.

Sparse trees.

Exposed stone.

Damaged mountain terrain.

Returning mountain wildlife.

Permanent Environmental Scars

Some dead trees remain.

Some ruins stay abandoned.

Some gravel fields remain.

Some waterways remain partly dry.

Large areas never return completely to their original appearance.

Design rule: Restoration creates a new version of nature, not an exact copy of the old world.

12. Endgame

The goal is not to restore every block in the infinite Minecraft world.

Instead, the player must restore several major ecosystems.

Possible final objective:

Reclaim a Plains region.

Reclaim a Forest region.

Reclaim a Swamp region.

Reclaim a Highland region.

Restore wildlife populations.

Activate a final Heart of the World structure.

After completing the main objective:

Restoration can continue.

The world remains playable.

Restored regions stay restored.

More biomes can be reclaimed.

Permanent environmental scars remain.
